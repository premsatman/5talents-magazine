## Instagram caption

Your feed says they found Noah's Ark. The team digging there says not yet.

Bear Grylls went to the Durupinar site in eastern Turkey. The post about his visit is sitting on
791,000 likes and his own account is on it as a collaborator, so the visit is real.

Read that caption slowly, though. It says he agrees Noah was the original survivalist. It never says
he confirmed anything about the ridge. That gap is where the whole week went sideways.

Then a separate account carrying Instagram's "AI-generated profile" label claimed soil samples had
exposed ancient marine life and artifacts "dating perfectly to the era of the great flood."

The team itself has been clearer than the internet. On 28 September, Noah's Ark Scans founder Andrew
Jones told Newsweek: "We have not identified any material as ancient wood or established its age."
The samples are still at the lab.

Their own posts hedge more than the reposts do. The drilling one said "believe could be." The soil
one called its reading a "working theory." The certainty got added by accounts quoting them.

Same day, archaeologist Dr Aaron Judkins, who led a 2013 Ararat expedition and believes the Bible,
wrote: "A claim like this is not merely sensational. It is irresponsible and unethical."

His line: "Good theology does not need bad archaeology."

Your faith was never resting on a ridge in Turkey. It rests on something already finished, and that
does not wobble while a lab result is pending.

Wait for the lab.

## Editorial notes
- Do NOT say Bear Grylls endorsed the site. His collab caption says he agrees Noah was the original
  survivalist. It does not claim he confirmed the formation. That distinction IS the story.
- The team's own three captions (Bear Grylls visit, drilling begins, soil samples) all hedge:
  "believe could be", "working theory", "can be sent to laboratories". The overclaiming is downstream,
  in aggregator and AI accounts and in headlines. Aim the piece there, not at the team.
- Ararat vs Agri: the top comments on the Bear Grylls post are Turkish users objecting to
  "Mount Ararat" instead of Agri Dagi. That is a national-identity dispute and we do not cover
  political controversy. Use "Agri province, Turkey" and leave the naming fight alone.
- Geography worth keeping: the site is NOT on Ararat. It sits on Mount Tendurek, about 29 km from the
  Greater Ararat summit, 1,966-2,004 m elevation, ~16 km southeast of Dogubayazit.
- Tagging @beargrylls in our post: his call. Accurate but it pulls his audience into a piece that
  partly corrects an impression about him.
