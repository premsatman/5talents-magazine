# Angh: Nagaland's Oscar hopeful asks if you can follow Christ and keep your culture

**Theja Rio's debut, set among the Konyak Naga in 1962, just won Toronto's Platform Prize. It isn't a Christian film, but it asks a question Indian Christians live with every day.**

> **What to know**
> - *Angh* is a 103-minute Konyak-language film by Nagaland director Theja Rio. It is his first feature.
> - It won the Platform Prize at the Toronto International Film Festival (TIFF) on 20 September 2026, the first Indian film to do so.
> - The win makes it eligible for Best International Feature at the 2027 Oscars. It has not been nominated.
> - There is no India release date yet.

*Angh* has just done something no Indian film had done before. Theja Rio's Konyak-language debut premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival on 15 September and won its Platform Prize five days later. Under the Academy's rules, that win makes it eligible to be considered for Best International Feature at the 2027 Oscars.

It is also a film about Christianity, though not the kind churches usually promote.

## What is Angh about?

It is 1962 in the Naga Hills. Anghbha, an ageing Konyak chief, and his son Amao, who cannot speak, are among the last people in their village still living the old way. The villagers who converted now live apart. They go to church, learn English and wear Western clothes. Then an American Baptist missionary arrives, played by Scottish actor Douglas Henshall. Most of the other actors are first-timers from Nagaland.

Around the family a new world is arriving: a road, medicine, school, hymns. The film watches what that does to a father and his son.

## Is Angh anti-Christian?

Not in the way the question is usually asked. Screen Daily reads it as an elegy for a community facing cultural erasure, and that grief runs through the reviews. But critics also say it refuses easy villains. The International Cinephile Society's review notes the missionary "is not presented simply as a villain," and that he brings medicine and education as well as a new faith. The Hollywood Reporter India puts it this way: "New worlds are opening up as old ones are being sealed shut."

So the film is critical of how the faith arrived, bundled with English, Western dress and a clean break from the village. It isn't an attack on Jesus. (We haven't seen *Angh* yet. This piece draws on the festival reviews, and we'll review the film itself when it reaches India.)

## The history behind it

Nagaland is one of the most Christian places on earth. In the 2011 census, 87.93% of its people were Christian, most of them Baptist. The first Naga believers were baptised at Molungkimong in 1872, among the Ao, through the work of American Baptist missionary E. W. Clark.

The Konyak came much later. Christianity reached most Konyak villages from the late 1950s, often carried by Ao Naga evangelists rather than foreigners, and some border villages converted as late as the 1970s, according to researchers Mark Woodward and Lemwang Chuhwanglim. Converts were often told to leave the morung, the village men's house, along with traditional ceremonies and ornaments. Decades later, Konyak Christians have been bringing parts of that culture back, from a heritage complex in Mon to traditional motifs on the clothes they wear.

## The question for Christians

*Angh* raises something the early church had to settle too: does becoming a Christian mean taking on someone else's culture? In Acts 15 the apostles met in Jerusalem to decide whether Gentile believers had to live as Jews. James answered "that we trouble not them, which from among the Gentiles are turned to God" (Acts 15:19, KJV).

The Bible's picture of the end isn't one culture either. John saw "a great multitude... of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues" before the throne (Revelation 7:9, KJV). Every tongue, Konyak included.

The gospel was never meant to arrive as a package deal with a necktie and an English hymnbook. Where it did, some things were lost that never needed to be. For young Indian Christians who still get asked whether they've "left" their culture, that isn't an old argument.

## Where can I watch Angh?

Not yet. *Angh* has only screened at festivals so far. Jungle Book Studios is handling distribution in India and abroad, and no release date has been announced. The Oscar shortlist for Best International Feature comes in December, with nominations in January 2027. We'll update this page when the film reaches India.
