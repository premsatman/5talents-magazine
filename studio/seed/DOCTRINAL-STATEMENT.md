## THE STATEMENT

### What we believe

Every magazine has a position whether or not it prints one. Here is ours, so you
know what you are reading.

**The things we hold with the whole church**

There is one God, who is Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

The Bible is the word of God and our final authority for what we publish.

Jesus Christ is fully God and fully man. He was born of a virgin, lived without
sin, was crucified, was buried, rose bodily on the third day, ascended, and will
return.

Every one of us needs saving, and none of us can manage it alone.

Salvation is a gift. It is received by trusting what Jesus has already done, and
it is not earned by anything we do afterwards.

The church is not a building or an organisation. It is everyone who belongs to
Christ, in every country and every century.

None of this is original to us. It is what Christians have confessed from the
beginning, and we would rather stand in that line than invent something.

**Where we put the emphasis**

What a magazine believes tells you what it holds. What it emphasises tells you
what it will be like to read. Ours comes to this.

**God has already done the thing.** The cross was not a down payment. What Jesus
finished, he finished, and the Christian life is spent discovering what is
already true rather than working towards something still owed.

**Righteousness is a gift and not a wage.** You are not more accepted on a good
week. You were never less accepted on a bad one. Standing with God is settled by
what Christ did, and nothing you manage or fail to manage moves it.

**Identity comes before behaviour.** Scripture almost never tells people to
change without first telling them who they already are. We try to write in that
order, because the other order produces exhausted people.

**Faith is receiving, not performing.** It is not a quantity of effort you
generate. It is taking God at his word about what he has already provided.

**The Holy Spirit is present help, not a doctrine.** He is God with us now, in
ordinary work and ordinary weeks, and we expect him to be at work in the lives
of the people we write about.

**God is good, and he is good towards you.** That is the sentence under
everything else here. Where a piece in this magazine has to choose between
making God sound severe and making him sound generous, we will get it wrong in
the generous direction.

**What we hold loosely**

Christians who agree on everything above still disagree, sincerely and in good
faith, about a great deal: church government, how and when baptism is done, the
order of events at the end, spiritual gifts and how they operate today, and much
else besides.

We have our own convictions on some of these. We do not make them a test of
fellowship, and we do not use this magazine to win those arguments. Our
contributors come from Baptist, Pentecostal, Catholic, Anglican, Church of South
India, Mar Thoma and independent congregations, and we would like to keep it
that way.

We publish about faith, work, culture, wellbeing and the history of the church.
We do not report news and we do not take political sides.

**How this shapes what you read**

We would rather print one honest sentence than three impressive ones. We say
where our information came from. We correct ourselves in public when we get it
wrong, and we have. And we try never to leave a reader feeling that God is
disappointed in them, because we do not believe he is.

## Notes for Premasis, not for the site

**On the Andrew Wommack alignment.** The emphasis section is the grace stream you
asked for, stated positively. Finished work, righteousness as a gift, identity
before behaviour, faith as receiving, the Spirit as present help, the goodness
of God. Anyone formed by that teaching will recognise it immediately. It is the
same theology already running through the rewritten Point of View column and the
`genzdevo` skill, so the magazine now says in its statement what it already does
in its pages.

**What was deliberately left out, and why.**

You asked not to make it more controversial, and the way to do that is not vague
language. It is choosing which questions to answer.

Left out: specific eschatology, whether tongues are the necessary evidence of
Spirit baptism, whether every believer can expect physical healing in this life,
church government, mode and subject of baptism, women in ministry, and anything
touching politics. Each of those splits sincere Christians, several of them split
Indian churches specifically, and none of them is needed to say what this
magazine is.

Two in particular are worth naming because they are distinctive to the stream
you are in. **Healing** and **prosperity** are where grace teaching most often
gets contested, and where an Indian magazine is most likely to be misread. The
statement therefore says "God is good, and he is good towards you", which is the
root of both and is unarguable, and stops there. It does not make a promise about
outcomes that a grieving reader could hold the magazine to.

**The paragraph that does the most work** is "What we hold loosely". A doctrinal
statement that only says what you believe reads as a fence. One that also names
what you will not fight about reads as an invitation, and it is the paragraph
that lets a Catholic contributor like Christine Lazares and a Pentecostal one
like Sheldon Bangera sit in the same issue without either being a compromise.
The denominations listed are the ones actually represented in your archive.

**One line to check before publishing.** "We correct ourselves in public when we
get it wrong, and we have." That is true now, and TRIAGE.md is the evidence, but
it is a promise you have to keep. If you would rather not commit to it in
writing, cut the clause; the sentence still works.

**This is a governance document, not just copy.** 5Talents is published by Sharon
Prayer Fellowship and Ministries, and Rev. K. Stephen is your advisor. A
statement of faith carrying the magazine's name should probably be seen by him
and by the board before it goes on the site, rather than being an editorial
decision alone.
