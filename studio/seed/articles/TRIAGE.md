# Archive triage

One row per piece in each issue, with a verdict. Anything not marked **publish**
is deliberately absent from the issue's `.json` file — this is where the reason
lives, so the same judgment does not get re-litigated in six months.

Verdicts:

- **publish** — original 5Talents work, rights ours, in scope
- **check rights** — probably syndicated or adapted; needs a source traced first
- **out of scope** — republishable, but outside the 2026 editorial scope
- **skip** — mostly graphic, or too thin to stand as a web article

### The health item is forty words

The whole of page 18 reads: *"Why intensely colored fruits and vegetables are
most beneficial to health? Next time you go to the supermarket, make sure to
choose fruits and vegetables with a variety of colors, ranging from yellow and
orange to deep red and purple."*

That is a filler box, not an article. Published as one it would be a thin page
of exactly the kind blueprint section 1 warns about — *"low value content" is a
common AdSense rejection reason* — and it would sit in the archive as a page
nobody benefits from reading.

The question behind it is a good one, though, and Wellbeing has nothing in it
yet. Worth commissioning as a real piece on why colour tracks with
phytonutrients, written for someone shopping in an Indian market rather than an
American supermarket. That is new writing, not archive conversion.

### On the Staines piece

Published at Premasis's direction, restructured as biography.

The scope argument still stands and is worth keeping visible. Blueprint section
4: *"5Talents does not cover persecution, caste, church–society controversy,
politics, or breaking news."* That line is on the about page and is your
evidence of scope if the IT Rules 2021 question is ever asked. An account of a
missionary and his two sons murdered by a mob is persecution content, however
historical.

What is live follows his life in sequence — Baripada, the leprosy home, the Ho
translation, the mat-weaving — and closes with January 1999 rather than opening
on it. The original led with the killings in its first paragraph. Nothing has
been added or removed; the order changed, and `archiveMeta.editNote` says so to
readers.

That keeps the man and the scope statement intact. If you would rather run the
original order, say so and I will restore it — but the about page line should
then be revisited in the same breath.

### The suicide-method edit, and why it is your call

Pavithran's testimony as printed describes the method of his suicide attempt in
physical detail — the cutting, the blood, the water changing colour. That
description has been removed. What is kept: that he was severely depressed, that
he went to the lake, that he tried to end his life, that he lost consciousness,
that he prayed, that he woke in a hospital bed. Nothing about the arc is softened.

The reason is not squeamishness. Method detail in a published account measurably
raises risk among readers who are already vulnerable — it is the one element
every safe-messaging guideline, WHO's included, asks publishers to leave out,
and the effect is strongest with young readers, who are the audience 5Talents is
being relaunched for. A magazine can carry the whole weight of a testimony
without carrying the instructions.

Three things follow from that decision, and they are yours to confirm:

1. **The article ends with a support note** and the Tele-MANAS number (14416).
   A testimony about a suicide attempt that offers no route to help is the
   version that does harm.
2. **`archiveMeta.editNote` records the change on the document**, so nobody
   later "restores" the original thinking it was lost in conversion.
3. **`interviewMeta.consentOnFile` is not set.** Pavithran is a named, living
   person and this is the most personal thing in the archive. Consent for a 2012
   print run is not consent for a permanent, indexed web page. Worth an email to
   him before this goes live — and it is a good email to send anyway, since he
   may have fourteen more years of story.

If you want the original text back, say so and it goes back — it is your
magazine and his testimony. But it should be a decision, not a default.

### The Missionary Story column is not ours

The George Muller piece is the English Wikipedia article on George Müller,

| Piece | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| **Graham Staines** (July, live) | English Wikipedia, "Graham Staines" | CC BY-SA — republishable *with attribution and share-alike*, never under our own byline |
| **Martin Luther** (August, live) | ["Martin Luther Biography" by Mary Fairchild, Learn Religions](https://www.learnreligions.com/martin-luther-biography-700828) | Commercial, all rights reserved — **no licence to republish at all** |
| **George Muller** (September) | English Wikipedia, "George Müller" | CC BY-SA, same problem |

The Luther piece is the serious one. Wikipedia at least has a licence we could
have complied with. Learn Religions is a commercially published site owned by
Dotdash Meredith with rights reserved, and the 5Talents text follows it sentence
by sentence — "Fearing for his life when a lightning strike narrowly missed him,
Martin cried out a vow to God", "his new found enthusiasm began to spill over
into his lectures and discussions with staff and faculty", the whole indulgences
passage. The boxed pull quote on the same page is separately lifted from
Wikipedia's Martin Luther lead. It has been live on the new site since the
August import.

**Both live pieces have been retracted.** `npm run retract` sets `retracted` on
the two documents with the reason recorded on each; every front-end query runs
through the `live` filter, the article page and sitemap included, so they leave
the site entirely rather than merely dropping out of the listings. The documents
stay in Sanity — deleting them would destroy the record of what was published
and for how long, which is the first thing anyone asks about afterwards.

Three things follow.

**Nothing under this byline gets published without a source check.** That means
every remaining issue: the Missionary Story column ran monthly, so there are
roughly fifteen more of these, and on the evidence the prior should be that they
are copied until shown otherwise. Ten minutes of searching per piece.

**This is not really about one person.** In 2012 a small volunteer magazine
copying an encyclopedia into a column was ordinary practice and nobody thought
of it as theft. Republishing it in 2026 on an indexed, permanent, advertising-
carrying website is a different act with different consequences, and the honest
framing when this comes up is that the standard changed, not that someone was
dishonest. Elizabeth Clayton is still credited as Creative Manager on the
masthead and may well be reachable — worth a conversation rather than a verdict.

**The two GotQuestions pieces confirm the other half of the pattern.** July's
"Should we raise our hands or clap during worship?" and September's tattoos
piece are both GotQuestions.org, the second one openly credited on the contents
page. Unsigned doctrinal Q&A in this archive should be assumed syndicated.

What survives all of this is the part that was always the point: the interviews.
Every Talent Search piece so far — Samuel Stanley Jones, Mary Sujani, Pavithran
Golconda, Bhaskar Rao — is original reporting, conducted by the magazine, with a
real person on the other end of it. That is the archive's actual asset, and it
is untouched.

### A GROQ trap, recorded

The retraction filter reads `coalesce(retracted, false) == false`, not the
obvious `retracted != true`. In GROQ a comparison against null evaluates to
null, and a filter treats null as false — so `retracted != true` would have
silently hidden every article predating the field, which is all of them. The
kind of bug that ships green and empties the homepage. Noted in AGENTS.md.

### Ramabai, rewritten

Written fresh from her own 1887 book and from Eric J. Sharpe's entry in the *Biographical Dictionary of Christian Missions*. Facts the copied version had wrong or missing:

- Her parents died in the **1874 famine** specifically, which is the fact that explains why she personally went out into the 1896–97 famine to collect children
- She sailed to England in 1883 **to qualify as a doctor**. Deafness ended it. Every retelling omits this, and it is the detail that shows she meant to come back useful, not to be rescued
- She was **already going deaf**, and stayed deaf, and ran Mukti anyway
- She made Sharada Sadan **explicitly non-proselytising** — a baptised Christian, funded by American Christians, who refused to require anything of the widows in her care. Her donors were uneasy and she held the line
- She taught herself **Hebrew and Greek in her sixties** and translated the Bible into Marathi from the original languages, setting the type on her own press
- Manorama died in 1921; Ramabai died nine months later

### One word to decide

The word "Hindu" appears twice in the rewritten piece, both times as the title of Ramabai's own book, *The High-Caste Hindu Woman*. Renaming someone's book is not an option, so the choice is to keep the title or to refer to it as "her first English book, published in 1887". Everywhere else the piece uses Brahmin, Sanskrit, the Puranas, caste and orthodox opinion, which is more precise writing in any case.

### The Andrew Murray reprint

Page 14 is a chapter of Andrew Murray's *Waiting on God* (1896), correctly credited to him. Murray died in 1917, so it is public domain and there is no rights problem at all — it is an honest reprint, not a copy.

It is still not our writing, and 1896 devotional prose is a hard sell to the readers this relaunch is aimed at. Left out for now. If you want a classic-reprint strand in the magazine it is a legitimate way to fill a slot, and it should be labelled as a reprint on the page.

### Images: the first good photograph in the archive, and we probably cannot use it

Page 4 carries a 2287 × 1988 photograph of Maria Clara in a garden holding a red canvas of dancing figures. It is the first image in four issues large enough for the overlay hero treatment, and it is properly lit and composed.

That is the problem. Every other archive photograph is around 560 px from a volunteer's camera. This one is a professional shoot — and the interview on the facing page says an AFP photographer took a particular interest in her paintings, and that the photographs ran in *Forbes*, *India Today* and others. Using an agency frame would be a straight repeat of the mistake this whole audit is about.

Imported as `textOnly`, no hero. **The fix is easy and worth doing:** Maria Clara is living, in Hyderabad, blogs her work, and her husband is a Times of India journalist. Ask her. She may have her own photographs of the paintings, and fourteen years of new work since.

### Carey, rewritten

Written from his own *Enquiry* and the nineteenth-century biographies. What the copied version left out, all of which is better than what it kept:

- He was apprenticed to shoemaking **because sunlight blistered his skin** and he needed an indoor trade
- Serampore was chosen because it was a **Danish colony**. The East India Company would not permit missionaries, so the whole enterprise ran for years out of a foreign enclave fifteen miles beyond British reach
- He was a **working botanist** — a five-acre garden, correspondence with Kew, published work on Indian flora, and the founding of the Agri-Horticultural Society of India in 1820
- **Serampore College**, founded 1818, chartered by Denmark to grant degrees, admitting students regardless of caste or religion. Still open
- The **1812 fire** destroyed the presses, the type for a dozen languages, and years of manuscripts including a polyglot dictionary. He started again
- When sati was prohibited in 1829 the edict reached him on a Sunday morning. **He skipped preaching and translated it into Bengali that day** so it could take effect without losing a week
- He was seventy-two when he died, not seventy-three as the source has it

And the part every version skips: **Dorothy Carey**. She did not want to go. A five-year-old son died of dysentery in a country she had no wish to be in, and she was severely mentally ill for about twelve years until her death in 1807. She is a real cost of this story and she did not choose it. It is in the rewrite.

### "Crossing the Bridge" — held on editorial grounds, not rights

The piece by "Mark" appears to be original, and it is not being held for provenance. It is being held because of what it says.

Its reading of Joshua 3 is that God physically lay down in the Jordan — "just as we do on a couch or bed" — and that the Israelites walked across the top of His body. That is not a metaphor in the writing; the author is explicit that it is what happened, and reasons from a science-lesson analogy about a wooden block displacing water.

That is an eccentric enough claim about the nature of God that publishing it under the magazine's name in 2026 would invite a doctrinal argument nobody needs. Held pending your view — it is your magazine and your theology, and it may be that the author would put it differently now.
