“Rapes in India”

You can put a stop to this

I

ndia is progressing, and so
are the crime rates with the
rise of rape cases. Today the
metros have problems; a rising
population certainly does not
help the cause. Of course, smaller
towns and rural area do not lag
behind, as the administration
tries to curb the menace of rising
crime rates. The National Crime
Record Bureau records a total of
23,582 cases reported in 2012.
Every other day we read or
hear about rape cases in
various parts of the country
through different media. But
it only affects the nation if it
is too brutal. This follows the
public rage in form of protests,
dharnas (strikes) and demand
for action. The politicians and
chief
administrators
starts
condemning the act. Most
people remember it for a while
and then have a ‘Chalta hai’
(what can I really do about
it) attitude. Most people are
back to their routines, as it
does not personally affect
them – until the next incident
happens. Last month’s rape
of a innocent 5-year old has
left the nation shaken again.
Why have we adopted the
‘Chalta Hai’ attitude? Do we
have any responsibility toward
this crime statics? Last week
our Pastor quoted a Bible verse

from Ecclesiastes 8:11, “When
the sentence for a crime is not
quickly carried out, people’s
hearts are filled with schemes to
do wrong.” (NIV) I was surprised
to read this verse. But it is true,
the criminals in India never get
punished immediately. The court
cases prolong for years and they
die without even going to jail.
The main reason for rapes is
not just weak laws, but a lack of
moral education, and increasing
materialistic
culture and
attitude which treats women
as pleasure giving objects.
Nudity on TV, pornographic
internet sites which are even
available on mobile phones and
movies brings out an insatiable
sexual desire that looks for
immediate gratification and
mental dissatisfaction, resulting
in these sexual assaults.
We have the responsibility to
make a difference as Christians.
We need to humble ourselves
and pray for a change in
our nation. As it is said in 2
Chronicles 7:14 “if my people,
who are called by my name, will
humble themselves and pray and
seek my face and turn from their
wicked ways, then I will hear
from heaven, and I will forgive
their sin and will heal their
land.” (NIV) Therefore, let us all
“Pray until something happens.”

or three years the idea formed and
grew in the mind of Aneesh Daniel,
transforming into a vision. For nine years
the foundational elements of the vision
have been nurtured, developed and refined
through research, correspondence, effort
and personal cost. For perhaps years to
come, but hopefully not many, the vision
will be tested and finally delivered for
people to enjoy experience and engage
with.

It is that goal to which Aneesh Daniel of
India and Andrew Matthews of Australia
have been working since their partnership
formed by a chance meeting thirteen years
ago. And it is that goal which took their
project in its infant form to San Antonio,
Texas, and the film awards.
The story of this part of the journey is a classic
one of God’s timing and intervention. The
San Antonio Independent Christian Film
w w w . 5 Ta l e n t s . i n

Festival (SAICFF) dubbed as the “Christian
Oscars” recognises the need to encourage
excellence in the planning stages of
as complex a project as a feature film.
Consequently an award is offered to the
Best Treatment – a three page document
outlining the story and business plan for
the proposed film.
They entered the competition in 2012,
and having not heard anything from the
organisers, they assumed the worst. As
it turned out, however, by the tyranny
of distance no doubt, they had missed
the deadline and had been entered,
unbeknownst to them, in the 2013
competition. It was therefore with some
surprise that they discovered they were in
the running for the award, and even more
surprised when they were announced
winners of the Best Treatment Award
for 2013 awarded by the San Antonio
Independent Christian Film Festival in Feb
of this year. More than 1,800 participants
from around the world were on hand in the
Alamo City for the presentation. But the
film makers are thrilled with God’s timing
and encouragement. So what is this story
idea that has earned a ‘green light’ from
the judges at SAICFF?
Tentatively titled Fire and Forgiveness,
the story is set against the background of
religious tensions that have emerged in
India over the past fifteen years. Aneesh
was becoming increasingly distressed
by the rising tensions between religious
groups in some parts of India, including a
neighbouring state to his own.
“I became captivated by the idea of a story,
a feature film that would truly speak into
this unpleasant situation” says Aneesh. The
film makers are adamant, however, that the
film is not “a disguised sermon”, or even a
parable. Instead it is a story, “at the core

of which is a personal tale about a man
who uncovers truths that challenge his
previously held beliefs.”
Andrew Matthews, the principal script
writer, expands, “You know, we all have
misconceptions and prejudices about
some things – we can’t know everything.
The question is whether, when faced
with facts and revealed truths, will we
change or adapt those beliefs, or will we
cling to them, refusing to be changed.
That’s what this story is about.”
The team does not, however, deny their
Christian perspective. While respecting
other religious views and beliefs, they allow
that as followers of Lord Jesus Christ, this
perspective must necessarily influence the
story. It is, after all, a story which features
characters who are Christians in a positive
light.
Indeed, of all the religious perspectives,
Christianity arguably offers the best reason
to forgive. The forgiveness for sin won by
Jesus on the cross enables His followers to
forgive in circumstances where forgiveness
defies mere human logic and emotion.
Aneesh began this journey with ten years
in Bollywood, working on feature films for
the likes of Mira Nair and Shyam Benegal.
Since then he has become an award
winning film maker in his own right. He
has produced eleven Television dramas in
this time, and currently airing on a national
network in India is the 26 episode serial,
Adventures in Odyssey, adapted from the
famous radio series initially produced by
Focus on the Family.
Furthermore Aneesh points out; this is not
merely a story for India, a contributing
factor no doubt in the honour bestowed
on the project as winner at SAICFF.

entertain, that it will strengthen those who
choose to stand in the face of persecution,

On the morning 20th February 2011,
Matthews says he was feeling discouraged
and prayed again for guidance. His
devotional that morning was relevant,
relating to goals and vision, but could just
have been coincidence. Then a prayer letter
concerning hundreds of Christian families
in India who had been displaced reminded
him of the relevance of this film project.
Finally a chance reading of a pastor’s
message crystallised his thinking – again
the message was about vision, this time
specifically addressing how dreams and
visions come to us, but with one important
addition: a warning - to be careful to go and
obey what He has said. The coincidence

was too much for Matthews to ignore. “To
pray for direction, then to ignore it when it
comes would be disobedience,” he says.
The latest encouragement, in the form of
the Best Treatment Award from SAICFF is
seen as confirmation from God to persist
through the next challenging phase of
seeking investment. The script will continue
to be polished, but the weight of evidence
indicates that this is a film that needs to be
made. Aneesh is now in search of partners
in order to launch the shooting of this film
at locations in India. Interested partners can
connect with Fire and Forgiveness at his
personal mail aneeshdaniel@hotmail.com

Mission Minute - Frontline Pastors, Ghana

J

ust last week, Global Advance welcomed
home a team from a week of ministry in
the west African nation of Ghana. The team
led by Global Advance’s own Jason Holland,
was the beginning of a long term process of
missions mobilization within this key African
nation. Already, Global Advance had invested
significant time and resources in Ghana through
the Marketplace Missions. The transformative
process that is taking place due to the training
of marketplace leaders and entrepreneurs
has been catalytic to encouraging holistic
transformation in many areas of the body of
Christ within Ghana.
Pastor Alfred of House of Faith Ministries as
well as Pastor Ernest have been instrumental
in opening doors of opportunity. Whilst
continuing the development of business leaders
through annual trips and ongoing projects,
House of Faith Ministries specifically requested
Global Advance to come alongside their vision
for mobilizing leaders for missions, training
pastors for church planting and encouraging
University students for their active participation
in the Great Commission. Global Advance has
gladly accepted their request - beginning with
a Frontline Shepherds Conference that was
held in late April in the central city of Kumasi.
Over 300 pastors, pastors wives and church
leaders came together for three days of
training, encouragement and prayer for the
advance of the Good News. Each leader came
to the meeting with expectations that only
the Lord could meet. Through clear surveys
of the audience, we found that about 40% of
the leaders attending had lost their passion

and zeal for ministry. They essentially were at
the place of ‘burn-out.’ By God’s grace and
through a time of prayer and encouragement,
these leaders committed to stay the course
knowing that they were not alone and that
God cared intimately for each one of them.
Additionally, those attending were challenged
to recommit to the original call of God and to
take their ministry to a new level of growth.
Specifically, we challenged them to plant
at least one new church within the next 12
months. Over 60 leaders stood in response
to this bold challenge, knowing the great
obstacles they face in the very near future. Our
last call of action was for those whom God was
calling to serve in cross-cultural missions. With
Ghana being surrounded by nations that are
predominately Muslim, the harvest is ripe for
harvest. Ghanians historically have only served
their own people through evangelism - but we
are believing that God will burden the hearts
of these Ghanian leaders for all who are lost,
especially those in closest proximity.
Global Advance is in continual discussion with
our partners in Ghana. We are already planning
the locations of future training events and the
mobilization of church planters for evangelism
in unreached areas. Thank you for your
continued support and prayer for those who
are serving on the front lines of the Gospel’s
advance.
Gratefully Commissioned,
Jonathan Shibley
Global Advance, President
