## B. The ten questions

**1.** You have been publishing as Learn Per Second since January 2018. What was the first app, and what made you sit down and build it? Was there a particular Sunday, or a particular person who could not find the page?

**2.** Mundari Durang Puthi has passed a hundred thousand downloads. Where did the text come from? Did you type the hymnbook out yourself, and did you have to ask the GEL Church for permission first?

**3.** Do you remember the first time you saw a stranger using one of your apps in a church?

**4.** Hymns of Malawi has over a hundred thousand installs. That is the one I did not expect. How does a developer in Odisha end up building a hymnbook in Chichewa for churches in Malawi? Who asked you, and how do you check a language you do not speak?

**5.** Your list runs across denominations — the GELC and NWGELC hymnals and catechism on one side, and Daily Catholic Readings, the Chaplet of St Michael and the Catholic Bible in Hindi and Tamil on the other. Most Indian Christians stay inside their own tradition. Did you set out to serve both, or did the requests just arrive?

**6.** Mundari, Kurukh, Odia, Hindi, Tamil, Chichewa. No company is going to fund a Kurukh hymnbook. What is the practical work of publishing in those languages — fonts, proofreading, someone to tell you a line is wrong?

**7.** The apps carry ads. I want to ask about the money plainly, because our readers are always told to build something for God and never told how it gets paid for. Do the apps earn anything? Do they cover their own costs? And what is your actual work, the one that pays?

**8.** A reviewer has asked you publicly to make the iOS version. Why have you not built it?

**9.** Your developer tagline on Google Play is Psalm 92:1. Fifteen apps, close to a million installs between them, and almost nobody knows your name. Does that bother you, or is it the point?

**10.** A young person in a small town in Odisha has a laptop and knows a little code, and wants to do something for God. Everyone around them is talking about music and preaching. What would you tell them to build?

## Notes for the editor

- **He volunteered the delay, so protect it.** He wrote that he is "a little busy with my work" and may need time. Follow-up in Notion is set to 11 October. Do not chase before then; a man who answers honestly about being busy is more likely to deliver than one who promises next week.
- **Question 4 is the find.** Hymns of Malawi was not in our original research and it is the most interesting thing in the whole portfolio — a tribal-language developer in Odisha quietly serving churches in southern Africa. If his answer is good, that becomes the headline rather than the Mundari app.
- **Question 7 is the one our readers need.** Ask it plainly and print the answer even if it is unglamorous. If the honest answer is that ad revenue is negligible and he funds this from a day job, say so; that is the useful truth for a nineteen-year-old weighing up whether to build something.
- **Question 5 could get delicate.** Serving Lutheran and Catholic churches at once is unremarkable to him and possibly remarkable to some of our readers. Let him frame it. Do not turn it into an ecumenism story he did not tell.
- **The lapsed domain is handled in the covering note, privately, and stays out of the published piece.** jesusprayer.co.in now redirects to a gambling page. Do not print the URL, do not link it, and do not make it a question — it would read as an accusation about something that is almost certainly just an expired renewal.
- **Numbers to confirm with him, not to print unchecked.** All install and rating figures here come from AppBrain and the Play Store listings: 15 apps, roughly a million installs in total, 4.41 average rating, four apps above 100,000 installs (Daily Catholic Readings 2027, Hymns of Malawi, Mundari Durang Puthi Gelc, Sakshi Vani Song Book Nwgelc). Third-party estimates and Play Store bands are both rough. Ask him for his own figures from the developer console.
- **He is from ODISHA, not Jharkhand.** He corrected us himself on 13 September, after the questions had gone out. We had inferred Jharkhand from GELC and NWGELC being Gossner Lutheran bodies; the inference was wrong. Jharkhand must not appear anywhere in the published piece. His Jelc Odia Christian Song Book app supports Odisha — JELC is the Jeypore Evangelical Lutheran Church. Ask which town, and which church he belongs to himself as opposed to builds for.
- **A connection to hold lightly.** JELC is the church in which Rev. Ms. M. G. Basanti, our January cover subject, was the first ordained woman. Two pieces in the same relaunch touching the same small Odisha church is worth knowing and might be worth telling him. It is not worth inflating into a link neither of them has claimed, and it must not become the frame of his piece — he is a developer, not a footnote to somebody else's story.
- **Also confirm:** surname spelling and preferred byline; his town; whether GELC and NWGELC are the right expansions; whether he works alone or has help; and whether learnpersecond.com is a live site we can link.
- **Sources used to build these questions:** AppBrain developer page for Learn Per Second; Google Play listings. Both are third-party or self-published, so everything here is a starting point for what he tells us, not a fact to print on its own.
