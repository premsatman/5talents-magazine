## The ten questions — draft, send only once he says yes

**1.** You have said Angh "felt like the only honest place to begin." Why this story first, and what would have felt dishonest?

**2.** You grew up after the change the film is about. What did you actually know of the old world before you started researching it, and what did you have to be told?

**3.** The film is in Konyak, and you are Angami. How did you work in a language that is not your own, and who kept you right?

**4.** Most of your cast had never acted and much of your crew had never made a film. What did you have to teach, and what did they teach you?

**5.** Douglas Henshall's priest is not a villain. Was that a decision you had to defend to anyone?

**6.** Nagaland is one of the most Christian places on earth. What has the reaction there been from pastors and church people who have heard about the film but not seen it?

**7.** Were you ever worried this would be received as an anti-Christian film? Is it one?

**8.** You call the transformation "remarkable and painful." Which parts are the remarkable ones?

**9.** 16mm, in the jungle, in a state with no film industry. What was the hardest day?

**10.** A young Naga who wants to make films and is being told to get a real job — what do you tell them?
