## 1. Arnel & Jeck Cadeliña — Malayang Pilipino Music

**To:** malayangpilipinomusic@gmail.com
**Subject:** Interview request from 5Talents Magazine, India

Dear Arnel and Jeck,

I am Premasis Satman, editor of 5Talents Magazine, a Christian magazine published from Hyderabad, India. We ran from 2012 to 2014, stopped when our daughter was born, and relaunch in January 2027. The archive is at 5talentsmag.com.

I would like to interview you, and the reason is an argument rather than a story.

You have said that if Filipino churches do not decide to make their own songs, they will be overwhelmed. India has exactly the same problem and almost nobody here has named it. Our churches sing songs written in Sydney and Atlanta, in English, and a great many young Indian Christians have never once worshipped in the language they think in.

I would like our readers to hear a Filipino making that case, because coming from another Asian country it will land differently than if I said it.

I would also like to ask about the beginning. You have described a relative arriving with two guitars to pray over your sister when you were twelve. That is where this started, and it is a long way from a national argument about worship.

Ten questions, sent ahead, answered in writing or by voice note whenever suits. You see the piece before it is published and we link whatever you would like linked. We would be grateful for two or three high-resolution photographs with a photographer credit.

No fee either way. We do not pay for interviews and we do not charge for coverage.

Why we are back after a long pause of our own: https://www.5talentsmag.com/faith/why-we-stopped-in-2014-and-why-we-are-back

Kind regards,
Premasis Satman

## 2. Gloryfall

**To:** info@gloryfall.com
**Subject:** Interview request from 5Talents Magazine, India

Dear Gloryfall,

I am Premasis Satman, editor of 5Talents Magazine, a Christian magazine published from Hyderabad, India. We ran from 2012 to 2014 and relaunch in January 2027. The archive is at 5talentsmag.com.

I would like to interview you about the translation work.

Getting permission from global publishers to put thirty worship songs into Tagalog is unglamorous, slow, legal work, and it is the most interesting thing anybody in Asian worship music has done recently. One of you put it plainly: an American's material struggle does not map onto a Filipino's.

India has the same problem and none of the same solutions. Our churches sing the same imported songs, in English, and nobody has done what you did.

I would like to ask how it actually worked. Who you had to ask, what they said, what it cost, and whether the translated versions are being sung.

Ten questions, sent ahead, answered in writing or by voice note by whichever of you wants to answer, or by several of you separately. You see the piece before it runs. Two or three high-resolution photographs would be very welcome, with a photographer credit.
