## B. Nine questions

**1.** The two hours that were most useful to me in Kuala Lumpur in 2020 were not the main sessions. They were the workshops — one on the responsibility of church and business leaders, one on sound and media management for churches. Do those practical tracks still run, and what is planned for 2027? I ask because that is the part of a conference our readers could actually use, and it is the part nobody writes about.

**2.** Melbourne in January and Kuala Lumpur in July 2027. Kuala Lumpur is the one closest to our readers, geographically and culturally. Why Malaysia, and who actually comes — locals, or people flying in from across Asia?

**3.** Do you have a sense of how far the songs travel? Our readers sing them in churches in India that you will never hear about, often translated, often without anyone knowing where the song came from. Is that something you track, or something you find out by accident?

**4.** A worship leader in an Indian town has a congregation of forty, one keyboard and no sound engineer, and they are leading a Planetshakers song on Sunday. What would you want them to know about leading it in that room rather than in an arena?

**5.** Related, and more bluntly: do the songs survive being stripped down? What is lost, and does it matter?

**6.** Most of our readers will never attend a conference like this — the airfare alone settles it. What is freely available to them, and what would you say to a leader who feels that the resources they need are always somewhere they cannot afford to go?

**7.** A conference produces a very high three days. What do you actually do to stop it being only that? What does the follow-through look like for someone who goes home on the Monday?

**8.** For whoever is answering on the music: where does a song start? Take one that people know and walk us through it from the first idea to the room.

**9.** A nineteen-year-old in India plays guitar at church and is starting to wonder whether this is what they are for. What would you tell them to do next — and what would you tell them not to do?

## Notes for the editor

- **Ask who is answering before finalising the questions.** That is the single most useful line in the reply. A media liaison will give you organisational answers; a worship leader or pastor will give you usable ones. Questions 8 and 9 are wasted on a press office and excellent with a musician. If Darryn offers someone specific, rewrite for that person.
- **The 2020 attendance is the strongest thing in this letter, and question 1 is now the best question.** You were at RAIN in Kuala Lumpur in January 2020 and sat in two workshops. That converts the approach from a stranger requesting access into a former delegate who knows what the event actually contains, and it gives you a question no press release would prompt: whether the practical tracks — church and business leadership, sound and media management — still run. Those workshops, not the main sessions, are the part an Indian reader could use.
- **Say only what you remember first-hand.** Your Facebook post from 24 January 2020 also mentions a conference called GLORY with dates that do not match the link it carries (the URL says malaysia-2021). Leave GLORY out. Name RAIN, Kuala Lumpur, January 2020, and nothing else you would have to check.
- **Disclosure, if the published piece leans on your own experience.** Attending a public conference six years ago is not a conflict of interest, and it needs no confession. But if the article quotes your own 2020 visit, one plain line saying the editor attended the 2020 Kuala Lumpur conference belongs in it — the same courtesy applied to the Basanti and Redwood pieces. Do not let personal recollection sit in the piece disguised as reporting.
- **Leave the private names out of print.** Joshua Lee who hosted you, and Anil and Sandeep who travelled with or guided you, are private individuals in a personal Facebook post. They are excellent context for you and have no place in the article or in the letter to Darryn without their permission.
- **Questions 3, 4 and 5 are the reason this is a 5Talents piece and not a listing.** Anyone can reprint a conference schedule. Asking a stadium worship movement whether its songs still work in a room of forty with one keyboard is a question only an Indian magazine would think to ask, and the answer is what our readers need.
- **Question 5 is the one they may not enjoy.** Ask it anyway, politely. If the honest answer is that the conference is not for people like our readers, that is worth printing too.
- **Keep the two pieces editorially clean.** Event listings are planned as a revenue line for the magazine. These reports are unpaid editorial, and they must stay clearly separated from any paid listing for the same events. If Planetshakers ever pays for placement, the label goes on the paid item and these pieces are not quietly folded into it.
- **Everything in writing.** This is the largest platform on the outreach list. Corrections, permissions and any comment they give should all be on email, not on a call.
- **Check the event facts against their public pages again just before publication.** Dates and venues for events fifteen months out move. Melbourne 7–9 January 2027 at the MCEC Plenary Theatre; Kuala Lumpur 23–24 July 2027 at Mega Star Arena.
