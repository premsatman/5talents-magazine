## B. The ten questions

**1.** A community church in Dimapur cast a six-year-old as the adult Jesus Christ. Whose idea was that, and what do you actually remember of it — the lines, the costume, the faces in the pews?

**2.** You have said that play "perhaps initiated" your journey into dramatic arts. When did it stop being a church activity and become the thing you intended to do with your life?

**3.** Getting from Dimapur to the National School of Drama in Delhi is not a small journey. What did it take, and what did your family say when you told them?

**4.** What was it like to be a Naga student at NSD? And was your church background something you brought with you openly, or something you learned to keep quiet about?

**5.** Advising young artists, you called the profession "the promised land laid down by our God for the Israeli people." I would like you to unpack that in your own words. Who should be occupying this territory, and what is keeping them out of it?

**6.** You founded Hill Theatre Nagaland. What did Nagaland not have that made you build it?

**7.** In most churches drama is decoration for the sermon — a sketch before the message. You are a professional who came out of exactly that. What does the church get wrong about drama, and what would you tell a pastor who thinks a skit is enough?

**8.** Has your faith ever made a role difficult to accept? Or a role made your faith difficult?

**9.** What is the hardest practical part of making theatre in Nagaland — money, venues, audiences, or actors who leave for something steadier?

**10.** A nineteen-year-old in Nagaland is good at acting and everybody around them is saying get a real job. What do you tell them?

## Notes for the editor

- **Question 5 is the spine and it needs care.** The quote — the profession as "the promised land laid down by our God for the Israeli people" — is the most striking thing in the file and the reason he is worth printing. He is treating the arts as territory to be occupied rather than a hobby to be excused, which is exactly the argument our readers have never been given. But it is a metaphor that can be misread, both theologically and politically, and we have it secondhand. Ask him to explain it himself, print his explanation rather than our reading of it, and do not headline the phrase without his gloss beside it.
- **Question 1 is the opening of the published piece, almost certainly.** A six-year-old playing the adult Christ is an image that carries the whole article. Get the concrete detail — who decided, what he wore, what he was asked to say.
- **Question 7 is where the piece earns its place.** Nearly every reader has seen a church skit. Very few have heard a professional who started in one say what the church misunderstands about the form. Let him be blunt if he wants to be.
- **Question 10 keeps the piece from being a profile of a distant successful man.** Nagaland readers especially will be weighing this exact decision.
- **Confirm before print:** his age — our note has him at 32 in an earlier interview, so roughly 40 now, and we must not print a guess. Also the name of the church in Dimapur; the year he graduated from NSD; whether Hill Theatre Nagaland is the correct current name and whether it is still running; and how he wants to be described, since actor, director, producer and founder are four different things.
- **Nagaland is now heavily represented** on our list — Nenti Kez, Abdon Mech, Mhademo Shitiri, Tabernacle, and others. Space these across issues rather than stacking them, or the relaunch will read as a Nagaland special.
- **If he never sends an email address, run the interview in the DM.** Losing a subject this good over a format preference would be a poor trade.
