## The questions

Easy ones first. The last three are the ones worth having.

**1.** Stephen got his first keyboard from your father. Do you remember the day
it arrived — and did anyone in the house know what it was starting?

**2.** He was playing in churches by the fifth standard and on a public stage by
the eighth. When did it stop being a family talent and start being a career?

**3.** Grade 8 piano from Trinity at sixteen, and one of only three NCC cadets
picked from Kerala for the Republic Day parade in 1998. How did both of those
fit into one teenager's week?

**4.** Years as a programmer before the performing — over 2,000 songs, more than
50 music directors. What does a programmer understand about music that an
audience never sees?

**5.** A. R. Rahman's work made him want to understand the engineering behind
the sound, and he started recreating those tones. What was the first one he got
right, and how long did it take?

**6.** He set Sanskrit slokas and verses from the Upanishads to western
arrangement for Sacred Chants. How did he think about that work as a Christian
musician — and did anyone ask him not to?

**7.** In 2010 the two of you turned the Chennai studio into a school. Why teach?
He was at his busiest.

**8.** What would he say to a nineteen-year-old who is technically excellent and
getting no work?

**9.** Ten to twelve shows a month, for years. What has that cost — in his body,
in family life, in the things he has had to say no to?

**10.** The verse on our 2013 cover was Philippians 4:13: "I can do all things
through Christ who strengthens me." It is usually quoted to mean *I will
succeed*. After twenty-five years of this, what does he actually think it means?

## Notes for Premasis

**Question 10 is the piece.** Whatever he says is the headline. A working
musician's honest reading of the verse we put on his cover is worth more than
any career summary, and it is the only question here that nobody else has asked
him.

**Send to Sam, not to Stephen.** Sam is the business, he replied to you quickly
in 2013, and artists at that level do not read their own enquiries.

**Timing.** Three to four weeks, then one polite follow-up. Then stop.

**If Bill Redwood is still reachable**, ask him to send a line first. He made
the original introduction and that is worth more than any amount of careful
wording from us.

**If the answer is no, or nothing comes back.** The fallback is a profile openly
labelled as compiled from Stephen's published biography and the public record,
with the sourcing stated at the top. That is a legitimate form and the 2013
cooperation supports it. What is not legitimate is the same material reformatted
and presented as our own feature.

## Wider point

This correspondence reversed a judgment in the triage. On the PDFs alone the
cover looked like copied text; the emails show a commissioned profile the
artist's own office supplied and illustrated.

**Check the 2012–14 email before treating any "held" verdict as final** — the
unsigned reviews, the Derksen profile, the conference reports, Olive Learning
Center. Bill Redwood was actively brokering stories through this period, so
material that looks unattributed in print may well have arrived with permission
attached.
