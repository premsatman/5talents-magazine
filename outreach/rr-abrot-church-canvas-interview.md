## B. The ten questions

**1.** Take us to the Saturday night that started this. You were an associate pastor who also had to make the Sunday slides. What were you actually doing, in what software, at what hour, and what was the specific thing that made you think somebody should build a better tool?

**2.** You sign yourself Co-Founder. Who is the other founder, how did the two of you meet, and what does each of you actually do? I want to get this right in print.

**3.** You have said you knew that to continue you would have to quit a full-time job at a very successful tech company, and that you were reminded God did not give us a spirit of fear but of power, of love and of a sound mind. What I would like to know is what happened in the room, not in the verse. Who did you tell first, and what did they say?

**4.** What did that decision cost? Money, months, anything you would rather not have lost. Our readers are handed stories about leaps of faith with the cost edited out, and they make worse decisions because of it.

**5.** You are still an active associate pastor. How do you hold both, week to week, and has either job ever made you bad at the other?

**6.** You built an AI design tool for churches. Some Christians are uneasy about AI touching anything to do with worship. What is your honest answer to a pastor who says a machine should not be making the image behind the sermon?

**7.** What is the most common mistake churches make with their graphics — the thing you see over and over and wish you could fix in one sentence?

**8.** Church Canvas Video is in beta and you have said you are starting a podcast about being a Christian entrepreneur in full-time ministry. Which of those two is the thing you actually want to do, and which is the thing you feel you ought to do?

**9.** This is the question our readers most need answered. A church in a small Indian town has fifty members, no designer, no budget, a slow connection and one laptop, and someone makes the slides late on Saturday night. Does what you built work for them, honestly? And if it does not yet, what would have to change?

**10.** A twenty-three-year-old is sitting in a church job, doing something badly because nobody else will do it, and starting to suspect they could build the thing properly. What do you tell them to do on Monday?

## Notes for the editor

- **The co-founder is the thing to fix first.** He signed "Co-Founder" and wrote "we" four times. If we publish this as one man's story we will have misrepresented a partnership, and he will notice. Question 2 and the paragraph in the reply both exist to correct our own error. Do not quietly drop them because the solo-founder version reads better.
- **Question 4 is the spine.** The leap-of-faith story is already public and told the way such stories are always told. What is not public is the cost. Our readers keep being sold the leap without the invoice, and this is the one question that makes the piece useful rather than inspiring.
- **Question 9 is what makes this ours rather than a US tech profile.** Ask it plainly and print the answer even if the answer is no. If he says the tool does not really serve a fifty-member church on a slow connection, that is a more honest and more interesting piece than a product endorsement, and it is the only version worth running in an Indian magazine.
- **Question 6 needs to be asked without an agenda.** A significant share of our readership is uneasy about AI. Let him answer, and do not editorialise around it afterwards.
- **Question 8 is a gentle one and may not land.** Founders are trained to say everything is intentional. If the answer is boilerplate, cut the question from the published piece rather than padding it out.
- **Confirm before print:** the eleven-thousand-churches figure, which came from our research and not from him; the co-founder's name and spelling; how he wants his own name rendered, since RR may be initials for something; whether he is still at the same church, and whether it should be named; and the correct capitalisation of Church Canvas and churchcanvas.ai.
- **Do not repeat the 2012 to 2014 line** from the original letter. The archive run is 2012 to 2017.
