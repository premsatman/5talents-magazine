# Latin America — 50 candidates, with a large caveat

Researched 15 September 2026, five parallel searches.

## Read this first

**Four of the five searches ran out of web-search quota and fell back on Wikipedia and direct site fetches.** Only the faculty search completed properly. The consequence:

- **Faculty: good.** Ten people, five verified personal or institutional emails, sixteen institutions, and language checked for every one. Usable today.
- **Musicians: weak.** No email found for anyone. Two entries are openly admitted placeholders that are far too big for us. Peru, Chile and Ecuador came back empty.
- **Authors: weak.** Skews to people in their fifties and sixties. No personal emails. One serious political flag.
- **Pastors: strong stories, no contacts.** Genuinely remarkable people, not one reachable email.
- **NGO: mixed.** Only four of ten reach operative faith. Several verified organisation inboxes.

**Recommendation: send the faculty letters now, and re-run the other four categories another day with fresh search capacity.** Do not treat the musicians and authors lists as finished.

## The language finding, which changes how we work here

This is the first region where language is a real obstacle, and the news is better than expected.

**Eight of the ten faculty read and write English.** Muzio has a PhD from Aberdeen and an MTh from Calvin. Barro has a PhD from Fuller and co-authored a book for Fortress Press. Both Roldáns have Oxford, Cambridge and Asbury residencies and publish through Langham. Brondos is American with a PhD from King's College London. Fields is American, published in English. Nelson Morales has a PhD from Trinity and an English monograph with Eisenbrauns. Karla Koll is American, Princeton and Union.

That is a straightforward list we can commission in English with no translation at all.

**The musicians and public figures are largely Spanish and Portuguese only.** If we want them we must budget translation, which is a real cost and a real delay.

So the faculty route is not just the best-yield here, as it has been everywhere. In Latin America it is close to the only route that works without extra machinery.

## Pastors — the best stories on the list, and not one email

**Erwin Cordón, Guatemala** is the standout of the entire Latin American search. A former Barrio 18 clica commander who once ordered killings by phone, whose own brother and best friend were murdered by rivals, who went to prison, and who now runs a gang-exit NGO in Zona 18 pulling thirteen-year-olds out of the pipeline he used to run. BBC Mundo sourced. **Verify identity carefully: a different, deceased Edwin Cordón exists.**

**Rolando Pérez Lora, Cuba** was arrested by State Security in a public park in March 2026, in front of his wife and young children, minutes after uploading a Bible teaching to YouTube. Corroborated across five outlets. His own YouTube channel, *Pregonero de Cristo*, is the only direct contact point anyone found.

**Pedro Palma, Chile** left a career as a financial executive in 2022 to pastor a dying church, and rebuilt it into a Chilean-Haitian congregation worshipping in Spanish, Haitian Creole and French. **Best English odds of the pastors**, through his US partner organisation.

**William Lacle, Colombia** fled Venezuela's collapse, then planted a church on the roughest hillside in Cúcuta. He and his wife handed out up to a thousand bowls of soup a day at the Simón Bolívar bridge during the pandemic.

**Esaú Aguilar, Mexico** pastors fifteen to twenty people in a village ten minutes from the cartel extermination site found in March 2025. Cartel lookouts follow his congregation when they go out. He packs tomatoes for a living.

Also: Leandro Poçam, ministering to punks and skinheads inside Paraisópolis. José Luis Urso, Rosario, who filmed drug bunkers police then declined to act on. **Ender Urribarrí, Venezuela — faith unverified, church name is common among prosperity congregations, do not commission without checking.**
