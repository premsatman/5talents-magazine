# Daily posts: a traffic engine alongside the magazine

v2 — 17 Sep 2026. Scanned relevantmagazine.com and instagram.com/relevantmagazine.
v1 aimed these posts at Indian readers; v2 goes global, which the data supports.

---

## 1. What RELEVANT is actually running

Two products under one masthead. **The magazine** — numbered digital issues
(currently 117), cover stories, podcast, R+ membership — is the identity.
**The daily stream** is the audience engine, and it is far cheaper than it looks.

Sections: Faith · Life · Culture · Justice · Current (Buzzworthy, Nation,
Opinion, World) · Slices · Mag · Podcast · R+

**Cadence, measured from their RSS feed, 3–15 Sep 2026:** 16 posts across 9
publishing days — about **1.8 a day**, weekdays only.

**The CDC tiredness post, pulled apart:** ~480 words · byline "RELEVANT Staff" ·
**one** linked source · no original reporting · **no faith angle at all** ·
hook, four or five stats, one wry closing line. A 30-minute job.

**The card:** masthead, section label in the corner, big headline, two or three
sentences of deck, stock photo. Recognisable at thumbnail size.

**The caption:** they give the whole story away and put the URL in plain prose at
the end. At 230k followers they monetise attention, not clicks. We must not copy
this — see section 5.

## 2. Going global is the right call, and our own numbers say so

Last 7 days in GA4, active users by country: **United States 50, India 49**,
then Sweden, Philippines, UK, Canada. In Search Console over 90 days the US
produced **18 impressions to India's 17**.

We are already read outside India roughly as much as inside it, on a site that
has barely started. Writing only for Indian readers would be aiming at half the
audience we have. **Global by default from here.**

This also fixes a revenue problem: Indian traffic earns roughly $0.50–3.50 RPM
against $12–40 for US traffic. Display ads will never fund this site on Indian
traffic alone.

### The line we still do not cross

We dropped news and current affairs deliberately, to stay clear of IT Rules 2021.
Going global does not reopen that door.

Off limits, no exceptions: Indian politics, communal incidents, persecution,
caste, elections, court cases, government policy. Also skip US political fights
wearing a faith costume — RELEVANT's `Nation` and `Opinion` beat. If a post would
need a lawyer, it does not get written.

Faith showing up in public is not politics. That distinction is the whole plan.

## 3. The four franchises — five posts a week, one per weekday

### WITNESS — 2 a week · the reach engine
Faith showing up in public, anywhere in the world. The name is deliberate: it
means both what the person did and what we did before publishing.

What qualifies: a team praying on the pitch after a final. An athlete's Bible in
the post-match shot. A barista who prayed with a customer in the drive-through.
A coffee shop that printed John 3:16 on its cups. A quote about faith in an
awards speech or a podcast.

**This beat is wide open right now.** The 2026 World Cup produced a run of these —
prayer circles, blessed boots, Bible studies between players — covered by Catholic
News Agency, EWTN, Deseret News and others. The "Jesus Won" apparel movement is
spreading across US pro sports. Winter Olympics athletes talking about faith.
That is months of material, global, and nowhere near Indian politics.

**Verification is mandatory, and it is our differentiator.** See section 4.

### FINDINGS — 1 a week
One research release, ~400 words, source linked, written for 18-to-30s.
Barna alone could carry this — State of the Church 2026, youth group attendance,
young adults driving a church-attendance resurgence. Then Lifeway Research, Pew's
Religion topic, Gallup, YouVersion's year-end Bible engagement data, State of the
Bible, and mainstream health or social data about 18-to-34s.

### THEN & NOW — 1 a week
**Ours, and nobody can copy it.** Take a number, claim or prediction from a
2012–2014 issue and check it against 2026. Already proved: *"In 2012 we printed a
page of YouVersion's numbers. It has since passed a billion installs"* was among
our top pages last week. Nineteen issues is a year of posts, every one of them
pulling readers into `/archive`.

### REMEMBER WHEN — 1 a week
Church-culture nostalgia. RELEVANT's Pokemon-sermons piece is the model. Zero
regulatory risk, high share rate. Global church memory plus Indian church
memory — conventions, choruses, VBS, the Christian bookstore — which no one else
is writing.

## 4. The verification rule — read this before writing a single WITNESS post

Viral faith clips are the most-faked genre on the internet. Staged content,
misattributed clips, old footage relabelled, and outright stunts.

**A live example.** The drive-through clip where a man holds up a Bible to be
scanned and says "Jesus paid it all" — that format has a documented dark twin. A
restaurant publicly named a customer who used the same line to walk out on a $17
bill. Run that one straight and we would have celebrated a man skipping his
cheque as a moment of witness. Meanwhile genuinely lovely versions of the same
story exist: a Starbucks barista who prayed with a customer in an Arkansas
drive-through, a Chick-fil-A employee who prayed for a customer, Vancouver
baristas photographed praying in a drive-through. Those got covered by real
newsrooms. The fakes did not.

**That is the test, and it is simple:**

1. **Has a newsroom with a masthead covered it?** Not an aggregator, not a faith
   meme page. If only reposters have it, we wait.
2. **Can we name the people and the place?** No names, no post.
3. **Can we find the original upload?** Reverse image search. If the earliest copy
   is a repost account, assume it is recycled until proven otherwise.
4. **Does anyone dispute it?** Search the story plus "fake", "staged", "hoax".
5. **If it fails and it is interesting anyway, write the other story** — *"This
   clip is everywhere. Here's what actually happened."* That post is better than
   the credulous one, and it is the reputation we want.

**Rights.** Embed, never rip. No downloading someone's Reel and reuploading it.
Credit the creator by handle in the post and the caption. We have a plagiarism
page on this site; behave like it.

**Why this matters beyond ethics.** Being the outlet that checks is the only
defensible position in a genre where a thousand accounts repost the same clip
within an hour. We cannot be fastest. We can be the one that is right — and that
is what an ISSN-registered publication is supposed to be.

## 5. The caption rule

RELEVANT completes the story in the caption. We must not.

- **Card:** headline plus one line. That is all.
- **Caption:** the setup and *one* beat, then the turn withheld — what happened
  next, who they turned out to be, whether it was real.
- **Link:** in the caption on Facebook, which is where our readers are.
- **Never** paste the full post as the caption.

The test: someone who reads only the caption should feel they are missing the
interesting half.

## 6. What the site needs

1. **A new section** for the stream — suggest `Witness` as the section name and
   the strongest franchise, with the rest as tags. Route plus listing page.
2. **Keep it out of the numbered monthly issues.** The ISSN application claims
   monthly frequency; the daily stream sits outside it.
3. **Outbound source link in every post**, followed, not nofollow.
4. **One internal link per post** into an archive piece or section page.
5. **Article schema** with a real `Person` author.
6. **Fix the key events.** GA4 records zero. Until newsletter signups fire, we
   cannot tell whether any of this converts — only that it drew traffic.

---

## 7. Two risks, named

**AdSense.** We are waiting on domain age to apply, and viral aggregation is the
weakest possible content in a reviewer's eyes. In v1 the originality hook was the
Indian read; going global, it is now **verification and context** — what we
checked, what we found, who these people actually are. A WITNESS post that only
restates a clip is not publishable. That rule is doing double duty: editorial
integrity and AdSense survival.

**Engagement without clicks.** This genre gets shared without being read. Watch
sessions per post, not likes. If WITNESS posts pull reach but no traffic, the
caption is giving too much away — tighten it before blaming the format.

---

## 8. Measuring it

Baseline, week ending 11 Sep 2026: 232 sessions this month; 68 organic social and
7 organic search sessions in seven days; 0 key events.

At four weeks:

- Sessions per post, by franchise. Which one is actually carrying?
- Do THEN & NOW posts move `/archive` pageviews? The one to watch.
- Organic social sessions against the 68 baseline.
- US share of traffic — going global should move it.
- Newsletter signups, once the key event exists.

Kill any franchise flat after six weeks. Do not keep all four out of tidiness.
